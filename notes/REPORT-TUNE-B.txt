NPS BEFORE / AFTER
------------------
Same harness, same 10 positions (opening, 4 middlegames, tactics, kiwipete,
promotion, endgame, open position), full move at a fixed node cap:

  before (original main.py, cap 25,000):   12.8k NPS    worst move 2.72 s
  after  (this main.py,    cap 100,000):   84.4k NPS    worst move 1.70 s

  => 6.6x nodes per second (12.8k -> 84.4k)

Note the original was slower than the brief's "~1 s/move": at 25k nodes its
worst measured move was 2.72 s, and mean throughput 12.8k NPS.

NODE CAP AND WORST-CASE MOVE TIME
---------------------------------
Cap: 100,000 nodes, fixed count, identical every move. No wall clock, no
randomness; the TT, killers and history are all reset each move, so a given
position always yields the same move regardless of call order (verified).

Measured on the shipped artifact over 135 positions harvested from random
legal games (spread over opening/middlegame/endgame), cap 100,000:

  worst move  2.46 s
  mean move   1.20 s
  overall     82.7k NPS

Cap sweep (135-position set, worst case):
   90k -> 2.2 s      100k -> 2.46 s      110k -> 2.91 s
100k was chosen as the largest cap that keeps the worst case comfortably under
the ~3 s target on this machine (hard env limit is 10 s). ~1.2 s of average
headroom per move also keeps the 1,800 s trial budget safe at ~3 s/move.

With the cap removed, the search completes depth 4 in the opening and quiet
middlegames and depth 6 in a sparse endgame before the cap bites; the original
reached depth 2-3 in the same positions at 25k nodes.

WHAT I CHANGED (all in the hot loop; eval and protocol unchanged)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
1. Cheaper move generation. The generator now emits packed integer moves
   (from | to<<7 | promo<<14) straight into one list instead of building
   (from,to,promo) tuples; and the check for a promotion rank is a single
   `to>>4 in (0,7)` test instead of two comparisons.
2. Capture-only generator `genc()` for quiescence. Previously qs() generated
   every legal move and then filtered; qs() now generates captures/promotions
   (and en passant) directly, so it no longer pays for quiet moves at all.
3. Cheaper move/unmove. Incremental eval, Zobrist hash and a material counter
   (wB/wb) are updated in the same pass; kings are tracked in `ks` so `chk()`
   needs no board scan (the original did `b.index(6)` per call).
4. Incremental Zobrist key + transposition table. The key is maintained by
   make/unmake (including castling rights and en passant), the TT is probed
   before generating moves, stores on cutoff with a bound flag, and supplies
   the TT move for ordering.
5. Move ordering: TT move first, then promotions, then captures by MVV-LVA,
   then killer moves (two slots per ply), then a history heuristic keyed by
   from*128+to. History is bumped only on a quiet cutoff, as before.
6. Repetition/50-move safety: the position key is pushed on a path list and a
   repetition scores 0 (the original compared whole board snapshots).
7. Iterative deepening with a node cap that raises TimeoutError; the root keeps
   the last fully completed depth's move. Perft-exactness is preserved because
   perft uses gen()/mv()/um() directly and never the search.
8. Crash guard in the env loop: think() is wrapped in try/except and falls
   back to the env's first legal move on an unexpected error, in addition to
   the existing "move not in legal_moves" fallback. It never resigns.

Determinism and safety checks run on the shipped bytes:
  - perft exact on all 6 positions, depths 1-3.
  - self-play soak (engine's own legal list, 15,000-node cap, random + search
    moves): 5 games / 159 plies, 0 illegal moves, king count intact.
  - think() repeated 3x on each of 10 positions: identical move and node count.
  - two independent fresh loads walking the same position sequence: identical
    moves at every step (replay determinism).
  - live env protocol check against the real practice server for the first
    game's first 4 moves (semi-slav opening): all 4 moves accepted as legal,
    then resigned to stop early. The scored trial was NOT run.

WHAT I DID NOT CHANGE
---------------------
Evaluation is byte-for-byte the same terms as the original (piece-square
tables, bishop-pair bonus). The env protocol loop is the same shape. This task
was speed and search quality only, not an eval rewrite.
